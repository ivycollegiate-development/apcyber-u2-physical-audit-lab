# apcyber-u2-physical-audit-lab — Campus Physical Security Audit

AP Cybersecurity Unit 2, Lesson 10.

**This is a print-and-walk audit. It is not a coding lab, and there are no computers on the walk.**

The whole packet is designed to be **printed**: `audit_checklist.md` is the sheet you carry, `audit_report_template.md` is the sheet you hand back, and `sample_completed_audit.md` is a worked example of what a good one looks like. Print all three, bring a clipboard and a pen, and leave the laptop at home.

---

# ⚠️ SAFETY RULES — NON-NEGOTIABLE

**Read these aloud with your teacher before anyone moves. Repeat the two that matter most: stay on public paths, and do not touch anything.**

1. **Stay on public paths and driveways.** Do not enter restricted areas, loading docks, or any space behind an access-controlled door. If the route goes somewhere you are not permitted, the route stops.
2. **Do not touch doors, badge readers, keypads, gates, fences, cameras, or windows.** You are observing the control, **not testing it.**
3. **Do not test access control.** Do not badge someone in, prop a door, follow anyone through a door, or ask staff to let you in somewhere. **A tailgating experiment in a real building is a real security incident**, and it will be handled as one.
4. **The teacher leads every route.** Stay with your group, stay in sight of the teacher, and **if the route stops, you stop.**
5. **Do not photograph other students, staff, or visitors. Do not write down anyone's name.** Note the behaviour, not the person.
6. **If you see an actual security problem on the walk, tell the teacher immediately and do not investigate it.** Not the stuck door you found. Not the unbadged person. Tell the teacher and move on.

**These are not rules to be tested or worked around. A rule you are unsure about means you stop and ask.**

## Your job

1. **Print** this README, `audit_checklist.md`, and `audit_report_template.md`.
2. In class, walk through the checklist and the rating scale with your teacher. Understand the scale before you walk — the scale *is* the assessment.
3. **Walk the route with your teacher.** Groups of 4, three route segments of about six minutes each.
4. **Rate every item independently.** Do not confer while you walk. You will compare afterwards, and the disagreements are the interesting part.
5. **Record for every item: the location, the rating, and one sentence of evidence.** "Missing" without evidence is an opinion. *"Missing: no reader on the roof access door, padlock on exterior"* is a finding.
6. **Reconcile as a group.** Where you rated differently, work out why. Bring the disagreements to the debrief — the debrief needs them.
7. **Write 3 prioritized recommendations** on the summary sheet. This is the deliverable.

## The rating scale

| Rating | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **P** — Present & Effective | The control exists and does what it is supposed to do. |
| **W** — Present but Weak | The control exists but is bypassable, misaligned, degraded, or gamed. |
| **M** — Missing | No control here, and a reasonable person would expect one. |

**"There's a camera, so it's fine" is not a rating.** Cameras have placement, blind spots, lighting, and retention — all four are on this checklist. Push on that in the debrief.

**Marking:** circle **P**, **W**, or **M** per item. Every item you mark needs one sentence of evidence, written at the time, in short handwriting.

## The routes

| Route | Covers |
|---|---|
| **A — Main entry sequence** | Approach path, front doors, visitor process, reception desk sightlines |
| **B — Interior circulation** | Corridor junctions, stairwells, roof access, emergency exits, where badges are required vs. assumed |
| **C — Perimeter and parking** | Fence line, bike area, delivery point, dumpster and loading zone, after-hours exterior lighting |

## Submitting

Hand in the completed checklist and the summary sheet at the end of the walk or at the start of the next class. Your report needs:

- Your completed checklist with ratings and evidence
- **3 prioritized recommendations**, each with: the control to add or change, **the specific finding it fixes**, and its priority — **justified by likelihood and impact**, the same two axes as the risk matrix from the previous lesson
- Both Taiwan addenda: **shared building network infrastructure risk** and **typhoon / flood physical resilience**

## Why this is a paper day

A laptop in your hand changes what you look at. You will look at the screen instead of the building. The physical security assessment this lesson is about is an *observation* skill — sightlines, lighting, the gap between a door and its frame, whether a camera is aimed at the thing it claims to cover. That is a trained eye, and you cannot train it while you are reading.
