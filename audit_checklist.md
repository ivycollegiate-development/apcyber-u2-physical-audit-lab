## A. PERIMETER

| # | Item | What good looks like | P | W | M | Notes / evidence (one sentence) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | Perimeter fence or wall is continuous | No gaps wider than a person, no loose panels, no damage | ⭕ | ⭕ | ⭕ | |
| A2 | Fence height and construction resist casual climb | ~2.4 m minimum; mesh small enough not to be a foothold | ⭕ | ⭕ | ⭕ | |
| A3 | Vehicle gate is closed, or visibly staffed when open | Gate should not stand open unattended during operating hours | ⭕ | ⭕ | ⭕ | |
| A4 | Gate hardware is not a known default (no spring-clip bypass) | Hinges, latch, and drop bolt on the *inside* of the frame | ⭕ | ⭕ | ⭕ | |
| A5 | Fence line is lit, or is in camera coverage | Dark fence corners are where a person stands unseen | ⭕ | ⭕ | ⭕ | |
| A6 | Parking / bike area inside the perimeter is covered | Anything with company property in it needs coverage or lighting | ⭕ | ⭕ | ⭕ | |
| A7 | Landscaping does not provide concealment near the perimeter | Shrubs and bins should not sit flush against the fence | ⭕ | ⭕ | ⭕ | |

## B. DOORS AND ACCESS CONTROL

| # | Item | What good looks like | P | W | M | Notes / evidence (one sentence) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| B1 | Exterior doors have a functioning closing device | Hydraulic closer, not a banged-shut door that has lost its arm | ⭕ | ⭕ | ⭕ | |
| B2 | Exterior doors have a strike plate / lock that resists prying | Long screws into the frame stud — a short screw set is a pry target | ⭕ | ⭕ | ⭕ | |
| B3 | No doors are propped, wedged, or held open | Check the ones that "always stick" — that is the one | ⭕ | ⭕ | ⭕ | |
| B4 | No door has an exposed or duplicated keypad code | Codes written on the frame, on a whiteboard, or shared in a group chat | ⭕ | ⭕ | ⭕ | |
| B5 | Emergency exits are alarmed or monitored if they are not badge-controlled | A free exit is a free exit *in* as well | ⭕ | ⭕ | ⭕ | |
| B6 | Emergency exits are not blocked by stored items | Boxes, bins, or delivery pallets in front of a crash bar | ⭕ | ⭕ | ⭕ | |
| B7 | Stairwell and roof access doors are controlled, not just assumed | Roof doors are the most commonly forgotten control on a campus | ⭕ | ⭕ | ⭕ | |
| B8 | Roof, plant deck, and mechanical access is locked with a keyed system, not a padlock with a shared key ring | A padlock whose key is on a shared ring is a padlock that is not locked | ⭕ | ⭕ | ⭕ | |
| B9 | Glass doors and sidelights are either rated or protected | Unrated glass next to a controlled door is a bypass | ⭕ | ⭕ | ⭕ | |
| B10 | Badge readers are at a height and position that suits the door | A reader mounted where a wheelchair user cannot reach is a control that excludes and does not protect | ⭕ | ⭕ | ⭕ | |

## C. VISITOR MANAGEMENT

| # | Item | What good looks like | P | W | M | Notes / evidence (one sentence) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | Every visitor is signed in, in writing or digitally, with a time **in** and a time **out** | Two timestamps, not one | ⭕ | ⭕ | ⭕ | |
| C2 | Visitors are issued a visible temporary badge or pass | A visitor walking with no badge is untraceable once inside | ⭕ | ⭕ | ⭕ | |
| C3 | Visitor badges are visibly different from staff badges | Colour, format, or wording — a distant observer should tell them apart | ⭕ | ⭕ | ⭕ | |
| C4 | Visitors are escorted, and the escort is accountable | Not "someone should walk them" | ⭕ | ⭕ | ⭕ | |
| C5 | Visitor records are backed up or retained, not just a paper sheet on the desk | A paper sign-in sheet is a single point of loss | ⭕ | ⭕ | ⭕ | |
| C6 | Deliveries and contractors are processed as visitors, not waved through | A delivery driver with a badge alone is a person you cannot name later | ⭕ | ⭕ | ⭕ | |
| C7 | Unescorted visitors cannot reach restricted areas without being noticed | Reception sightlines — can the desk actually see the corridor behind it? | ⭕ | ⭕ | ⭕ | |

## D. CCTV COVERAGE

**Judge cameras against a stated purpose, never by count. "There is a camera" is not a rating.**

| # | Item | What good looks like | P | W | M | Notes / evidence (one sentence) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | All entry points are covered | Every exterior door, including the ones nobody uses | ⭕ | ⭕ | ⭕ | |
| D2 | The loading / delivery point is covered | Loading areas are unmonitored more often than any other space | ⭕ | ⭕ | ⭕ | |
| D3 | Parking, bike, and walkway areas are covered or lit | Company property outdoors needs eyes on it | ⭕ | ⭕ | ⭕ | |
| D4 | Cameras are aimed at the *approach*, not at the wall beside the door | A camera covering the door frame tells you about a wall | ⭕ | ⭕ | ⭕ | |
| D5 | Field of view has no large blind spot immediately at the door | The 2 m in front of a door is where a person waits to be let in | ⭕ | ⭕ | ⭕ | |
| D6 | Night / low-light legibility is acceptable | Infrared or enough light to identify a face, not just a shape | ⭕ | ⭕ | ⭕ | |
| D7 | Recording is continuous, not motion-gated with gaps | Motion detection misses a person who stands still | ⭕ | ⭕ | ⭕ | |
| D8 | Retention meets the stated need, and you know what it is | 30 days is standard; ask what incident it would still support | ⭕ | ⭕ | ⭕ | |
| D9 | Video timestamps are synchronised to other systems | An un-synced DVR cannot be correlated with a badge log at all | ⭕ | ⭕ | ⭕ | |
| D10 | Live view is monitored, or the monitoring is honest about not being monitored | A recorder nobody watches is evidence, not detection | ⭕ | ⭕ | ⭕ | |

## E. MONITORING AND LOGGING

| # | Item | What good looks like | P | W | M | Notes / evidence (one sentence) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | Badge events are logged per-credential, with door and timestamp | "Someone badged in" is not a record | ⭕ | ⭕ | ⭕ | |
| E2 | Badge holders have individual credentials — no shared badges | A shared badge has no owner and cannot be revoked | ⭕ | ⭕ | ⭕ | |
| E3 | Access rights are granted per role and reviewed on a schedule | Someone should be able to say when access was last reviewed | ⭕ | ⭕ | ⭕ | |
| E4 | Logs are retained long enough to be useful for an investigation | 30 days, or 90, or "we don't know" | ⭕ | ⭕ | ⭕ | |
| E5 | Log storage is separated from the system that produced the log | Otherwise anyone with admin can clear the evidence | ⭕ | ⭕ | ⭕ | |
| E6 | There is a documented incident response path for a physical alarm | If nobody knows who gets called at 3 AM, the alarm is decorative | ⭕ | ⭕ | ⭕ | |
| E7 | Alarms at controlled doors are monitored or auto-notified | A local siren nobody hears is not a detection control | ⭕ | ⭕ | ⭕ | |
| E8 | Patrol activity is recorded with times and locations | A log of who walked where and when is what closes a blind spot | ⭕ | ⭕ | ⭕ | |

## F. ENVIRONMENTAL AND POWER RESILIENCE

**Taiwan-specific. This section is where a campus audit usually finds the most.**

| # | Item | What good looks like | P | W | M | Notes / evidence (one sentence) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| F1 | Servers, network gear, and archives are above flood level | Note the *floor*, not the intent. A ground-floor server room is a finding | ⭕ | ⭕ | ⭕ | |
| F2 | There is a water or moisture sensor in any flood-exposed equipment space | Detection is the control when prevention is a building-level decision | ⭕ | ⭕ | ⭕ | |
| F3 | Roof membrane, flashing, and drainage look maintained; no pooling | A flat roof in typhoon season is a scheduled outage | ⭕ | ⭕ | ⭕ | |
| F4 | Exterior lighting is adequate and working on the approach and at the loading area | Look for dark stretches between light fittings | ⭕ | ⭕ | ⭕ | |
| F5 | Emergency / battery lighting is present and tested, not just installed | "Installed" and "still working after 18 months" are different claims | ⭕ | ⭕ | ⭕ | |
| F6 | UPS or generator coverage extends to the systems that must survive an outage | 15 minutes of UPS on the network core is not continuity — name what it covers | ⭕ | ⭕ | ⭕ | |
| F7 | Records storage is protected from water, and is not in a single location | Paper and tape are not backed up. Tape is a backup that floods too | ⭕ | ⭕ | ⭕ | |
| F8 | Shared-building infrastructure (risers, comms room, loading dock) is access-controlled on your side | If you lease it and your neighbour reaches it, the control is your neighbour's decision | ⭕ | ⭕ | ⭕ | |

### Rating reference

**P — Present & Effective:** the control exists and does what it is supposed to do.
**W — Present but Weak:** the control exists but is bypassable, misaligned, degraded, or gamed.
**M — Missing:** no control here, and a reasonable person would expect one.

*Design flaw or human process?* When you mark **W**, try to say which. A door that cannot be made to latch is a **design** flaw. A door that is propped because the closer is broken and nobody will fix it is a **human process** flaw — and the second kind is usually cheaper to fix.
